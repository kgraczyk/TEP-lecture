## About the Course

This repository contains lecture slides for *Theory of Elementary Particles*, a graduate-level course taught at the University of Wrocław since 2015.

The course provides a systematic introduction to the theoretical foundations of particle physics, with particular emphasis on symmetry principles, quantum field theory, and the construction of the Standard Model.

Rather than presenting the Standard Model as a collection of established results, the lectures focus on the theoretical reasoning and mathematical derivations underlying its structure. Fundamental concepts are developed step by step, connecting mathematical formalism with physical observables and experimental evidence.

A distinctive feature of the course is its emphasis on explicit calculations, including the derivation of scattering cross sections, the construction of group representations and hadronic wave functions, gauge theories, spontaneous symmetry breaking, and fermion mass generation.

## Course Content

The lectures cover the following topics:

1. **Foundations of particle physics:** Relativistic kinematics, scattering processes, the S-matrix, and particle classification.
2. **Scattering theory and quantum field theory:** Cross sections, decay rates, phase space, and Feynman rules.
3. **QED scattering and discrete symmetries:** Scattering amplitudes, leptonic and hadronic tensors, parity, charge conjugation, time reversal, and CPT.
4. **Lie groups and representations:** SU(2), SU(3), tensor products, weight diagrams, and Young tableaux.
5. **The quark model:** Hadron classification, flavor symmetry, baryon wave functions, color, and magnetic moments.
6. **Deep inelastic scattering:** Structure functions, Bjorken scaling, the parton model, and parton distribution functions.
7. **Gauge theories and QCD:** Noether's theorem, local gauge invariance, Yang–Mills theory, and quantum chromodynamics.
8. **Weak interactions:** Fermi theory, the V–A structure, and the motivation for electroweak unification.
9. **Spontaneous symmetry breaking:** Goldstone's theorem and the Higgs mechanism in Abelian and non-Abelian gauge theories.
10. **The electroweak Standard Model:** SU(2) × U(1), gauge boson masses, electroweak currents, and Higgs couplings.
11. **Flavor physics:** Cabibbo mixing, the GIM mechanism, the CKM matrix, and CP violation.
12. **Neutrino physics:** Dirac and Majorana masses, the seesaw mechanism, neutrinoless double-beta decay, and neutrino oscillations.

## Prerequisites

The course is intended primarily for graduate students in physics. Familiarity with quantum mechanics, special relativity, and basic concepts of quantum field theory is recommended.

## Lecture Materials

The repository contains PDF versions of the lecture slides. The materials have been developed and revised over several years of teaching.

The slides are intended to accompany lectures and emphasize mathematical derivations. Some intermediate steps and physical interpretations are discussed during classroom presentations and may not be fully documented in the slides.
